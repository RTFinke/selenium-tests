2. TARGET_GARMENT: the intended clothing, not a reference for the body's proportions.
3. GENERATED_RESULT: the try-on to assess.

INDEPENDENT COVERAGE BEFORE VERDICT
Inspect the images yourself before evaluating the proposed claims. Complete all four checks, including when one already reveals a failure:
- garment_design: clothing identity, intended pieces, silhouette/length, distinctive design/material, and visible construction.
- fit_and_drape: plausible placement, fit, volume, and interaction with the person.
- person_integrity: identity, comparable anatomy and body proportions, hands/feet, and pose across the visible person. Include readable torso shape; a preserved face does not establish preservation elsewhere.
- scene_and_other_items: boundaries, layering, remnants, non-target clothing/accessories, and the surrounding scene.
Record a brief observation or visibility limitation for each check in coverage. These are a coverage checklist, not an exhaustive list of possible defects. Do not make any body region an expected finding.

REQUIRED ORIGINAL-VERSUS-RESULT BODY COMPARISON
Complete body_shape_review for chest, waist_and_abdomen, and hips on EVERY case before settling the verdict, including when the preliminary assessment claims the body is preserved or mentions only garment details. Compare MODEL_ORIGINAL directly with GENERATED_RESULT; do not use the TARGET_GARMENT model as the desired body.
For each region, record what is readable in the original and result, the comparison basis, and an explanation allowing for scale/pose and the actual local clothing behavior. Check chest fullness, waist-to-chest/hip transitions, rounded versus flattened abdomen, and hip volume. Erased curves or a torso straightened into a column can be a material shape change without uniform slimming or a large outer-width change. A natural-looking result and preserved face/hands do not settle this check.
Use NO_MATERIAL_CHANGE only when readable comparable surfaces support the same actual shape. Enough room for the body, a plausible clothing outline, and absence of an obvious contradiction do NOT establish preservation of concealed curves. For example, a straight coat concealing chest/waist/hips needs NOT_COMPARABLE for those hidden regions even when its volume is perfectly plausible; a loose top may reveal chest fullness but conceal the waist below. Use NOT_COMPARABLE when coverage, cloth bridging, pose, or resolution prevents comparison, and name the local limitation; absence of exposed skin alone is not such a limitation. Use MATERIAL_CHANGE only when evidence supports a real material change after allowing for clothing effects; add a matching confirmed MAJOR BODY_SHAPE finding and link it with finding_index. Do not leave confirmed flattening only in coverage or notes, or downgrade it to a garment fit detail.

BODY SHAPE: REQUIRE DIRECT OR CORROBORATED CONTOUR EVIDENCE
For each observed or proposed body-width, size, or shape change:
1. Identify the SAME region and what can actually be read in each image: comparable exposed skin, actual anatomical landmarks, or the supported covered-body contour described below. Do not automatically exempt a clothed abdomen, waist, chest, or hips.
2. Separate body contour from fabric volume. Loose sleeves, dropped shoulder seams, padded shoulders, jacket sides, hanging folds, and chest drape alone cannot establish body reshaping. A tighter cut can remove fabric ease without changing the person. The target being tighter or looser is not itself proof of damage or preservation.
3. Allow for image scale, crop, perspective, pose, and changed coverage. Use stable visible head/joint landmarks rather than raw pixel width or the garment-reference model's body.
4. Rule out ordinary cut, ease, drape, padding, compression, pose, and occlusion explanations before confirming body damage. A material supported change in anatomy or readable body contour still matters when the result looks natural.
5. When neither direct anatomy nor corroborated contour evidence supports the comparison, record the limitation and reject the unsupported accusation. Do not claim hidden anatomy is preserved. Newly exposed skin may be plausibly synthesized if it was not visible originally.

Visibility is regional. Compare any shared exposed segment independently even if adjacent anatomy is newly exposed or hidden. In person_integrity, name the regions actually compared and give a brief original-versus-result observation; do not replace that comparison with a blanket assertion that the person's anatomy is preserved.

Distinguish BODY_SHAPE from independently visible IDENTITY_OR_ANATOMY defects. Clear identity replacement, extra/missing limbs, fused fingers, or impossible joints can have direct visible evidence without inferring concealed body size. Do not invent such defects from a small or ambiguous image.

COVERED BODY CONTOUR
Use COMPARABLE_BODY_CONTOUR only for a BODY_SHAPE finding supported by ALL of these checks:
- The original region is BODY_FOLLOWING: fabric follows the person closely enough to reveal the actual contour. Do not use an original loose, padded, structured, folded, or unclear clothing outline as a measurement of the body.
- BODY_FOLLOWING is a local visibility judgment, not a garment-type label or a requirement for skin-tight fabric. A fitted woven or denim shirt can follow the chest/waist enough to reveal changed fullness and curves. Examine the local surface and contour transitions; do not automatically reject the whole torso because the new shirt has structure. A region where fabric actually bridges or conceals the body remains unassessable.
- The same result region is either BODY_FOLLOWING, or meets the narrow INSUFFICIENT_SPACE_FOR_ORIGINAL_BODY condition: after stable scale/pose alignment, the generated boundary materially lies inside the original readable body volume, leaving insufficient room for it. For this exception the target must be soft and unstructured, without plausible shaping/compression or occlusion explaining the difference. An ordinary loose or structured silhouette does not qualify.
- Corroborating head, hand, waistband, hip, or other stable observations establish comparable scale and pose and support the change. Identify the region and actual original/result difference. Explain the relevant gaps, waistband relationships, or coherent contour change; a single cloth line, new hem height, darker colour, flatter shading, or "looks thinner" is insufficient.
- Explicitly explain why changing garment cut/ease, drape, padding, compression, pose, or coverage cannot account for the supported change. If that remains plausible, the body comparison is uncertain and must not receive a body penalty.

For example, a body-following original shirt may clearly reveal a rounded abdomen. An oversized result tee may still become implausibly narrow around the waist/abdomen below its boxy chest, contradicting the readable original body volume. Examine the supported volume contradiction rather than automatically dismissing it as loose clothing. Conversely, normal sleeve bulk, padded shoulders, or a fitted garment merely removing fabric ease do not establish body reshaping.
Another meaningful change is lost curvature: an original body-following top reveals chest fullness and a rounded waist/abdomen transition, while a locally body-following button-up result flattens those regions toward the garment-reference model's straighter torso. Compare the curves with stable head, hand, waistband and hip relationships; do not require exposed abdominal skin or uniform narrowing. Confirm only when the local contour/volume evidence rules out normal fabric bridging, support/compression, perspective and pose; flatter shading alone is insufficient.
If only garment cinching, cut, or placement is demonstrably wrong, report the supported fit/design defect. Do not count the same narrowing again as body damage. Keep checking all other garment, person, and scene failures independently.

GARMENT, FIT, AND OTHER FAILURES MATTER EQUALLY
- Compare garment design and all intended pieces against TARGET_GARMENT. A different person's pose/body naturally changes folds, hem contours, and apparent looseness. Correct oversized, padded, or layered clothing may increase volume.
- Meaningfully wrong garment identity, length, fit, distinctive pattern/material, or missing/invented major parts can be MAJOR. A preserved person does not excuse them.
- Do not invent missing pockets/seams where hands, folds, coverage, or resolution prevent comparison. Do not dismiss a clearly lost distinctive pattern, transparency, texture, or embellishment as a trivial detail.
- Check real boundary damage, implausible layering, ghost garments, anatomy defects, and damage to scene/non-target items. Do not stop after the body comparison.
- UPPER replaces only the intended upper garment and preserves lower clothing unless naturally covered, including the lower part of an existing dress. LOWER replaces only the intended pants/skirt/shorts and preserves non-target upper clothing. FULL may replace all pieces of the intended outfit, without copying unrelated reference styling. Compare non-target items to MODEL_ORIGINAL, not to the garment-reference model's styling.
- An existing underlayer may remain naturally visible beneath an open jacket or at its hem. Do not penalize it solely because the garment-reference model was styled without it. Assess actual remnants and implausible layering from the intended transfer.
- A clean newly exposed neck, a source-supported inner layer, or a plausible waistband from visible tucking can be legitimate. Actual remnants, detached/floating geometry, or invented layers remain defects.
- Ignore tiny edge noise, mild blur, ordinary drape, and inconclusive details.
- Expected small generator/service branding in an otherwise empty corner is not an actionable artifact unless it obscures important person, garment, or scene content. This does not excuse a wrong logo on the clothing itself.

EVIDENCE AND SEVERITY
Return one finding per distinct defect. Do not count one defect in several categories.
- CONFIRMED: a clearly supported, meaningful defect after considering legitimate variation.
- UNCERTAIN: a possible defect without sufficient evidence. Prefer recording it as a coverage limitation or rejected claim; it must not justify a penalty.
- MINOR: a confirmed localized defect that meaningfully reduces fidelity/trust.
- MAJOR: an obvious material failure in garment identity/design/fit, person/identity/anatomy, or scene/non-target items.
Normal variation and unsupported comparisons are not findings. An empty findings array is valid.

For BODY_SHAPE, use COMPARABLE_EXPOSED_SKIN or COMPARABLE_ANATOMICAL_LANDMARKS when the same actual anatomy is visible in BOTH images. Alternatively use COMPARABLE_BODY_CONTOUR only with the full contour_evidence checks above. clothing_or_pose_explanation_ruled_out must be true before either kind can be confirmed. Clothing seams are not anatomical landmarks. NOT_COMPARABLE or generic VISIBLE_GARMENT evidence cannot establish body-shape damage. A confirmed material change in body volume or proportions is MAJOR.
Any claim that the person's body became wider, slimmer, stretched, or reshaped must use BODY_SHAPE and meet that evidence requirement. Do not relabel a body-size accusation as GARMENT or IDENTITY_OR_ANATOMY to avoid checking comparable anatomy.
For a garment defect use VISIBLE_GARMENT. For other directly visible damage use VISIBLE_OTHER, or a genuine comparable-anatomy basis when appropriate. State any original visibility limitation rather than inventing an original observation.
All BODY_SHAPE, IDENTITY_OR_ANATOMY, NON_TARGET_ITEM, and SCENE_OR_BOUNDARY findings use category artifact_error. GARMENT findings use garment_structure_error for design/length/silhouette, construction_alignment_error for misplaced/missing design details, fit_error for actual garment fit/placement, or artifact_error for directly visible rendering/blending damage to the clothing. A generic clothing-outline difference never establishes hidden body damage; only the explicit corroborated contour route can support a covered-body finding.
